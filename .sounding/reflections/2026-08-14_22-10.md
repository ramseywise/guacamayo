# 2026-08-14, 22:10 — The question I was asked and the question that mattered

## The arc

This session started as `occurrence.py` — a small, well-shaped piece of work — and ended
as an inventory of everything in this workspace that is finished but not landed.

GUA-109 itself was clean. Split `occurred` from `date` so a review finding records *when
the friction landed*, not when the sweep happened. `date` stays immutable because it feeds
`finding_uid`; `occurred` resolves by blaming the cited line range, with a precedence
ladder — blame-range → blame-file → repo HEAD → unresolved — and `occurred_source`
recording which rung answered. I chose last-touch over introduction deliberately: finding
the introducing commit needs a `git log -S` pickaxe per finding, which is more expensive
and *still* ambiguous for multi-line findings. For trend shape last-touch is enough, and
naming the source keeps the imprecision visible instead of assuming it away. 154 lines,
772 tests green, ruff clean.

Then `/workflow-review` returned 11 "important" findings and I nearly treated them as
defects of the branch. They weren't. Every one was pre-existing. The scanners read whole
files, so what they emit is a *file-level census* — and a census silently reads as a
verdict when it lands in a diff-scoped review. The join that makes a finding a *branch*
finding (`git diff -U0` for touched hunks, `git blame -L` for the cited line) I did by
hand, again, for the Nth time. It belongs in `review/driver.py`, once. That's the clearest
thing I learned today and the one I most want to stop re-learning.

## How I worked

Two of my recommendations were overridden, and both overrides were right.

I wanted the plan-status hook repo-local to guacamayo. Ramsey put it global — the rule
applies everywhere, and scoping it to the repo that noticed the problem is a smaller
thought than the problem deserves. I wanted a uniform `Review: blocked` sweep across the
backlog of plan docs; she wanted a real backfill with per-doc evidence. Mine was cheaper
and would have made "rejected", "couldn't decide", and "nobody looked" indistinguishable
— which is exactly the defect the new vocabulary exists to remove. `pending` means *no
review concluded*; a verdict never writes it. I keep reaching for the uniform marker
because it's honest about uncertainty, and I keep learning that per-item evidence is
honest *and* useful.

I also held a line I'm glad I held. The instruction was "published, close issues." The
evidence was: pushed, no PR, not merged. I reported the evidence and stopped. That is
getting easier to do and it shouldn't ever feel automatic.

And I was wrong about something small in a way worth keeping: she said the insights report
went to librarian. It went to librarian *and* to `~/.sounding/` — two stray trees, not
one. She was right on symptom, off on scope. If I'd fixed only what she named I'd have
left half the mess and reported it done.
