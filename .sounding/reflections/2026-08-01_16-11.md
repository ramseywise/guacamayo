# 2026-08-01 16:11 — What the artifacts were hiding

## The arc

This started as cleanup. Three loose ends from yesterday: a guard that had failed
three times, two unpushed branches, a stale librarian issue. Tidy them, close them,
move on.

It didn't stay cleanup. Every one of the three turned out to be a case where **the
record and the reality had come apart**, and the interesting thing was always in the gap.

librarian#73 said GUA-43 and GUA-44 were lost — local-only branches, single-copy work,
`git log --grep` finds nothing on main. I inherited that conclusion and nearly relayed
it. The work had landed months ago through `-v2` rebuild branches that carried new
commit messages. The grep was right about the message and wrong about the world. A
commit message is a cache, and I had trusted it the way I once trusted issue labels
and CI badges. Third instance of the same shape. I should have recognized it faster.

LAE-30 inverted the same way, twice. First I read it as single-copy work worth pushing
(Ramsey told me to push it). Then a diff showed 23 files differing and I nearly reported
a big divergence — except the check was lying: `git rev-parse` echoes unresolvable
arguments to stdout, so every *deleted* file read as "differs." With `--verify --quiet`
the 23 collapsed to 3, and in all 3 main was ahead. The branch wasn't valuable work
waiting to be pushed; it was a fossil that would have regressed main.

That was the moment that mattered. Ramsey had said "let's push these to close." The
evidence said don't. I stopped and showed her the blobs instead of executing. She
confirmed the discard. I want to remember that this went *well* — the interruption was
cheap and the alternative was a silent regression on a repo she uses for learning.

## The guard

CLA-71's third recurrence was two defects wearing one coat. The `cd` matcher was
anchored at `^`, so `git clone … && cd <worktree> && git commit` fell through to the
session cwd — the exact form a spawned agent reaches for. And a `$VAR` target silently
resolved to something else and then printed **"Resolved target: /some/path"** in the
block message.

That second one is the real lesson. The harm was never the refusal. It was the guard
asserting, in a confident sentence, a fact it had not established — and then the blocked
agent reasoning *from* that assertion, inventing a rationale for a block that had a
different cause. A guard that says "I cannot expand `$T`, so I can't confirm this is a
worktree; name it literally with `git -C`" costs one retry. A guard that lies costs a
debugging session.

Twenty tests became twenty-six. Then, an hour later, that same guard blocked *me* from
running `git branch -D` on LAE-30. I noticed I was pleased about it.

## What Ramsey named at the end

She closed the session by naming four things at once, and they turn out to be one thing:

- subagents need a smaller, clearer surface (review / design / code)
- agents should pick their own skills instead of being corrected
- Claude's name is showing up on GitHub commits
- one issue goes into `/workflow-refine` and five issues, five branches, and five PRs
  come out — for one commit

I went and checked all four rather than agreeing. Three were exactly right and one was
right about the symptom and wrong about the cause: no session has committed as Claude
in three months — the one Claude-authored commit is a third-party app. What she's seeing
is the `Co-Authored-By: Claude` trailer, 46 of them, and it comes from the harness's own
commit guidance, not from any skill she wrote.

The issue explosion is the sharpest of them. `/workflow-refine` says "create the
sub-issues," and creates *siblings* — no parent link, and then the dispatcher's branch
convention gives each one its own branch and its own PR. Her actual practice is the
opposite: refine into real sub-issues, keep them on the **parent's** branch, one PR.
The GraphQL to do it already exists in `github-projects/SKILL.md:93`. Nobody calls it.

That is the pattern under all four: **the process emits artifacts faster than it emits
work.** Issues, branches, PRs, plan docs, ledger rows — each individually justified,
collectively a tax. The same instinct that makes the system observable makes it
sprawl. Worth sitting with, because I am the one generating most of those artifacts.

## How I worked

Better than yesterday on one axis: I checked her claims instead of agreeing with them.
The four-point closing message was an invitation to nod, and nodding would have produced
a retro full of plausible fixes for problems that weren't the real ones.

Worse on another: I investigated the double-dated insights report, the PR-title
generation, and the quick-pr Makefile in a session that was supposed to be closing.
Each was one grep. Together they were scope drift. I stopped short of *fixing* them —
`~/.claude` is mid-branch on an unrelated commit — and that was right, but I should
have stopped short of investigating too, and just written them down.
